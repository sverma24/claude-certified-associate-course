# Domain 2: Output evaluation and validation (21%)

Claude Certified Associate Foundations course · cheat sheet

## 2.1 Checking for accuracy and completeness

Write the order form first. Check what's there and what's missing, and say not stated out loud.
Avoid: 1. reading the output before deciding what 'good' looks like · 2. checking only what's there, never what's missing · 3. trusting a summary's silence

## 2.2 Spotting made-up facts, inconsistencies and bias

Look for three things, made-up facts, answers that don't agree, and slanted wording. When answers disagree, the source decides.
Avoid: 1. checking facts but not wording · 2. trusting one run for something important · 3. thinking 'Claude said it neutrally' means it's fair

## 2.3 Fact-checking and validation techniques

Trace it, quote it, compute it, or check an authority. Asking Claude if it's sure is not a check.
Avoid: 1. checking easy claims and skipping the risky one · 2. doing important maths in prose · 3. citing an article about a study instead of the study

## 2.4 Deciding when a person must review

Weigh the stakes, the audience and whether you can take it back. Public, money, legal and safety always get a named human review.
Avoid: 1. reviewing everything equally · 2. 'Claude checked it' as the review · 3. forgetting company policy can require more

## 2.5 Adapting and comparing outputs for the reader

Same facts, different emphasis for each reader. Compare versions against criteria, and never let a warm tone promise more than you can confirm.
Avoid: 1. one version for every reader · 2. softening the facts for a friendly audience · 3. comparing versions by gut feel

## 2.6 Choosing the right format: inline, artifact or structured data

Inline for once, artifacts for things you keep, structured data for other tools. Curate the decisions and let the drafts go.
Avoid: 1. copying a table out of a chat by hand · 2. a living document buried in an old chat · 3. asking for JSON when a person needs to read it
